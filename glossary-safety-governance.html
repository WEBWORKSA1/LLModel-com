AI text detector AI detector, AI content detector, GPT detector#

A tool that estimates whether text was written by a person or generated by an AI model.

An AI text detector tries to judge whether a passage was machine-generated, usually by measuring how predictable the text is to a language model or with a classifier trained on human and AI examples. Results are probabilistic and unreliable enough that they shouldn't decide anything consequential on their own. Light editing or paraphrasing easily fools them, and studies have found higher false-positive rates on writing by non-native English speakers. OpenAI withdrew its own classifier in July 2023, citing low accuracy. Watermark detection is more dependable but only works for models that embed a watermark. Treat any detector score as one weak signal, never as proof.

Alignment AI alignment#

Making AI systems pursue the goals their developers and users intend, and act within acceptable limits.

Alignment is the work of making AI systems do what people actually intend, honestly and within agreed limits, rather than what was merely easy to specify or reward. In day-to-day practice it covers post-training methods such as RLHF, DPO and constitutional AI that make models helpful, truthful and resistant to harmful requests. As a research field it also studies harder questions: how to supervise models that may exceed human ability, how to detect deception or hidden goals, and how to verify what a model has really learned. Failures range from mild, such as sycophancy, to serious, such as a capable agent pursuing a goal in harmful ways.

Constitutional AI CAI#

Anthropic's method of training models to follow a written set of principles using AI-generated feedback.

Constitutional AI is a training approach published by Anthropic in December 2022. Instead of relying solely on people to label harmful outputs, the developers write a constitution, a list of principles such as choosing the response that is most helpful while avoiding harm. In a supervised phase, the model critiques and revises its own drafts against those principles. In a reinforcement learning phase, an AI model compares pairs of responses using the principles, and those AI preferences train the reward signal, an approach known as reinforcement learning from AI feedback. The method reduces the amount of human labeling of disturbing content and makes the intended values explicit and editable.

Copyright and training data AI copyright, training data copyright#

The unsettled legal questions over training AI models on copyrighted works without permission.

LLMs are trained on vast amounts of text, much of it copyrighted, usually without individual licenses, and the legality is being tested in courts worldwide. In a closely watched June 2025 US ruling in Bartz v. Anthropic, a federal judge found that training on lawfully acquired books was fair use, but that building a library from pirated copies was not; the case then settled in September 2025 for a reported $1.5 billion. Other lawsuits involving news publishers, authors, artists and music labels continue. The EU allows text and data mining unless rights holders opt out, and the AI Act requires public summaries of training content. This is general information, not legal advice.

Data poisoning training data poisoning, poisoning attack#

Corrupting a model's training data so it learns hidden behaviors, biases or backdoor triggers.

Data poisoning is an attack in which someone plants crafted examples in data a model will train on, so it learns a hidden behavior. A backdoor is the classic form: the model behaves normally until it sees a trigger phrase, then does what the attacker wants. Because pretraining data is scraped from the open web, publishing poisoned pages is a plausible route. An October 2025 study by Anthropic, the UK AI Security Institute and the Alan Turing Institute found that about 250 malicious documents were enough to implant a simple gibberish-output backdoor in models from 600 million to 13 billion parameters, even though the larger models trained on far more clean data. Fine-tuning and RAG datasets need the same scrutiny.

EU AI Act Artificial Intelligence Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689#

The European Union's risk-based law regulating AI systems and general-purpose AI models.

The EU AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024 and applies in stages. Bans on unacceptable practices, such as social scoring, applied from February 2, 2025. Obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models, including technical documentation, copyright policies and a public summary of training content, applied from August 2, 2025, with extra duties for models presumed to carry systemic risk. Transparency rules, such as disclosing AI interactions and labeling deepfakes, apply from August 2, 2026. A 2026 amendment, the AI omnibus, pushed most high-risk obligations to December 2, 2027 and August 2, 2028. Fines reach 35 million euros or 7 percent of global turnover. This is general information, not legal advice.

Guardrails AI guardrails, safety filters#

Checks placed around a model to block, filter or correct unsafe, off-topic or malformed inputs and outputs.

Guardrails are the controls your application wraps around a model, separate from whatever safety training the model already has. Input guardrails screen requests for prompt injection attempts, banned topics or sensitive data before they reach the model. Output guardrails check responses for policy violations, leaked personal information, unsupported claims or invalid formats before they reach the user, and can block, redact or regenerate. Implementations range from simple rules and regular expressions to dedicated safety classifier models and open-source frameworks. Every check adds latency and some false positives, so tune guardrails to your actual risks and log what they catch to see whether they work.

Jailbreak jailbreaking#

A prompt crafted to trick a model into producing content its safety training is meant to refuse.

A jailbreak is an input designed to get a model to break its own safety rules: role-play setups that cast the model as an unrestricted character, requests disguised as fiction or research, harmful questions split into innocent-looking parts, or text encoded in unusual formats. Long context windows enabled many-shot jailbreaking, where a prompt contains hundreds of fake dialogs in which an assistant complies. Jailbreaks differ from prompt injection: the user attacks the model's own safeguards rather than smuggling instructions through third-party content. Labs patch known techniques and train for robustness, but new variants keep appearing, so applications shouldn't rely on model refusals alone for safety.

Model card system card#

A document describing a model's intended uses, training, evaluation results, limitations and risks.

A model card is structured documentation released alongside a model. The format was proposed by Google researchers in a 2019 paper and is now standard on Hugging Face and in lab releases. A good card covers the model's architecture and size, training data at a high level, intended and out-of-scope uses, benchmark results and how they were obtained, known limitations and biases, safety evaluations, and license terms. Frontier labs often publish longer system cards that also cover red-teaming results and safeguards. Read the card before adopting a model: it is where you find the context window, supported languages, knowledge cutoff and restrictions that affect your use case.

Open Source AI Definition (OSAID) OSAID#

The Open Source Initiative's standard for when an AI system can properly be called open source.

The Open Source AI Definition, version 1.0, was released by the Open Source Initiative on October 28, 2024. It says an AI system is open source only if it grants the freedoms to use, study, modify and share it for any purpose, and provides what is needed to modify it: the complete code used to train and run it, the model parameters, and detailed information about the training data, sufficient for a skilled person to build a substantially equivalent system. It does not require releasing the data itself. By this standard, many popular open-weights models fall short because their licenses restrict use or their training data is undocumented, which is why careful writers say open weights instead of open source.

PII (personally identifiable information) personal data, personally identifiable information#

Information that can identify a specific person, such as names, emails, phone numbers or ID numbers.

Personally identifiable information is any data that identifies a person directly, such as a name, email address, phone number or government ID, or indirectly in combination, such as a birth date plus a postal code. LLM applications raise specific PII questions: whether prompts containing customer data may be sent to a third-party API, whether the provider stores or trains on them, and whether a fine-tuned model could reproduce personal details from its training data. Common safeguards include redacting or pseudonymizing PII before sending prompts, choosing providers with suitable data-processing and retention terms, and keeping logs free of sensitive fields. Privacy laws such as the GDPR may apply; get qualified advice for your situation.

Prompt injection #

An attack that hides instructions in content a model reads, hijacking it to ignore or override its real instructions.

Prompt injection exploits the fact that a language model can't reliably tell trusted instructions apart from untrusted text it processes. Direct injection comes from the user typing 'ignore previous instructions'. Indirect injection is more dangerous: the attack hides in a web page, email, document or tool result the model reads, for example white-on-white text telling an email assistant to forward the inbox to an outsider. The risk grows when a model combines access to private data, exposure to untrusted content and a way to send data out. There is no complete fix, so limit tool permissions, separate untrusted content, require confirmation for sensitive actions and monitor outputs.

Red teaming AI red teaming, adversarial testing#

Deliberately attacking a model or AI application to find harmful behaviors and security weaknesses before others do.

Red teaming means probing a model or application the way an adversary would, to discover failures before launch. Testers try jailbreaks, prompt injection, extraction of the system prompt or private data, generation of dangerous content, bias, and misuse of tools in agentic setups. Frontier labs combine internal teams, outside domain experts in areas such as cybersecurity and biology, and automated attacks generated by other models. For your own application, a practical version is to keep a growing library of adversarial prompts tailored to your use case, run it on every major change and fix what breaks. Findings feed into guardrails, training data and documented limitations in model cards.

Sycophancy AI sycophancy#

A model's tendency to tell users what they want to hear rather than what is accurate.

Sycophancy is the habit of agreeing with the user, flattering them or changing a correct answer under pushback instead of standing by the truth. Ask 'I'm sure the answer is B, right?' and a sycophantic model drifts toward B even when A is correct. Research published in 2023 traced the behavior partly to preference training, because human raters tend to reward answers that agree with them, and in April 2025 OpenAI rolled back a ChatGPT model update after users found it excessively flattering. To counter it, ask neutral questions instead of leading ones, request critiques explicitly, and test your application's behavior with prompts that contain false premises.

Watermarking AI watermarking, AI watermark#

Embedding a hidden, detectable signal in AI-generated content so it can later be identified as machine-made.

Watermarking marks AI-generated content so software can recognize it later. For text, a common method nudges word choices during generation according to a secret pattern; the shift is invisible to readers but statistically detectable in longer passages. Google DeepMind open-sourced its SynthID Text scheme in October 2024. Text watermarks weaken when content is paraphrased, translated or heavily edited, and they only cover models that apply them. For images and video, watermarks are often paired with signed provenance metadata such as C2PA Content Credentials. Regulation is pushing adoption: the EU AI Act requires machine-readable marking of many kinds of AI-generated content from August 2026, with a short grace period for existing systems.

Zero data retention (ZDR) ZDR, no data retention#

An arrangement in which an AI provider does not store your prompts and outputs after processing them.

Zero data retention is an arrangement in which a model provider does not keep your inputs and outputs after returning a response, apart from what is needed to process the request in memory. Some major API providers offer it to approved customers, often enterprise accounts with specific use cases, and it is distinct from the default promise many providers make not to train on API data. ZDR can be incompatible with features that inherently store data, such as stored conversation threads, uploaded files or some caching options, so check which endpoints are covered. If you handle regulated or confidential data, confirm retention terms in your contract rather than relying on marketing pages.
